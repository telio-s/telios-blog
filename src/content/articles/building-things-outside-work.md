Building outside of work feels different because no one hands you the exact requirement.

You have to decide what matters, what to ignore, how polished the first version should be, and when to stop improving one detail so the whole thing can move forward.

That kind of work teaches more than tooling. It teaches taste. It teaches restraint. It shows whether an idea is still interesting after the first excitement fades.

It also makes you more empathetic toward business owners. An idea can feel clear in your head, but the moment you try to build it, hidden decisions appear everywhere.

That is one reason I care about requirements and scope. They are not paperwork. They are the bridge between intention and reality.
