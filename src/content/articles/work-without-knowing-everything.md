Early in my working life, I thought competence meant already knowing the answer. Over time, I learned that real work is often more ambiguous than that.

Sometimes the useful contribution is asking the question no one has made explicit. What are we trying to solve? Who is affected by this decision? What happens if this assumption is wrong?

Not knowing everything is not the problem. Pretending that uncertainty does not exist is the problem.

The more I worked with software, the more I saw that good outcomes depend on making unclear things discussable. That applies to technical design, business scope, client conversations, and personal growth.

I still value expertise. But I trust it more when it is paired with humility, careful listening, and the willingness to clarify before acting.
