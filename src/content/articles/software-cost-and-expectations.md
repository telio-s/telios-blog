People often compare software quotations as if they are buying the same finished object from different shops. In reality, two quotations can describe very different assumptions.

One vendor may include requirement clarification, testing, deployment, training, and support. Another may quote only implementation based on the initial feature list. Both can look reasonable, but they are not pricing the same work.

This is why scope matters. If the scope is unclear, a cheaper quotation can become expensive later through revisions, missing features, or misunderstandings.

Cost control starts before development. It starts with deciding what problem matters most, what the first version should include, and how delivery will be accepted.

The goal is not always to choose the lowest price. The goal is to understand what you are actually paying for.
